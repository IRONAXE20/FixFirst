# RentBasket Appliance Troubleshooting SOP

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 25 October 2026  
**Purpose:** A practical, phone-first Standard Operating Procedure for RentBasket customer support, field technicians, and replacement decisions.  
**Priority appliance brands:**
- **AC:** Lloyd, Voltas
- **Refrigerator:** Whirlpool (primary), Lloyd
- **Washing machine:** Whirlpool
- **Microwave:** Haier, Panasonic, Midea

> **Operating principle:** Diagnose the symptom; do not guess the failed component. Guide only safe, external checks that a customer can perform without tools or opening the appliance. If the issue is not resolved quickly, create a service ticket. RentBasket decides repair versus replacement after applying the current internal policy.

---

## 1. How to use this SOP

This document is designed to be used during a live customer call or WhatsApp conversation.

1. Identify the appliance, brand, model, and exact symptom.
2. Run the **Safety Gate** before any troubleshooting.
3. Open the matching playbook using the issue index below.
4. Give **one instruction at a time**. Ask the customer to report the result before moving to the next step.
5. Stop as soon as the issue is resolved.
6. If the approved checks do not resolve the issue, classify the case and arrange a technician or replacement assessment.
7. Record the symptom, steps tried, result, final diagnosis, cost, downtime, and whether it recurred.

### 1.1 Priority brands and model-specific instructions

This SOP covers common household units from the listed brands, but product designs differ by model and by AC type (split/window), refrigerator type (direct-cool/frost-free), and washing-machine type (top-load/front-load/semi-automatic).

- Ask for a photo of the appliance nameplate/model sticker if the model is unknown. Do not ask the customer to move a heavy appliance to find it.
- Use the product's own manual if a button, mode, filter, drain, or reset procedure differs from this SOP.
- Never assume all Lloyd, Voltas, Whirlpool, Haier, Panasonic, or Midea models use the same sequence or error-code meaning.
- Do not make claims about a brand's failure rate from online complaints. Record RentBasket's own results to identify actual repeat faults.

### 1.2 Warranty and rental policy

RentBasket has indicated that there is no warranty after one season for the relevant rental stock. Support staff must still check the appliance's model, purchase/installation records, and any applicable manufacturer coverage or internal service agreement before promising a charge, free repair, or exclusion. Do not automatically tell a customer that a manufacturer's warranty has expired based only on the appliance's age or rental start date.

## 2. Standard call workflow

### Step 1 — Open the case

Suggested opening:

> “I’ll help you check a few safe things first. I’ll guide you one step at a time. If it does not resolve the issue, I’ll arrange the appropriate service support.”

Capture:
- Ticket ID
- Customer name and registered phone number
- Rental order/contract ID
- Address, tower/flat, access instructions, and preferred visit window
- Appliance category, brand, model, serial number if accessible, and approximate age
- Date/time issue first occurred
- Exact customer statement in their own words
- Photos/video where useful and safe
- Whether the appliance is currently running
- Whether the issue has occurred before, and any prior technician visit
- Any immediate safety concern

### Step 2 — Ask the four diagnostic questions

1. **What exactly happens?** (No power, no cooling, water leak, noise, no water intake, etc.)
2. **When does it happen?** (Immediately, after a few minutes, only during spin, after the door is closed, etc.)
3. **Did anything change recently?** (Power cut, moving the appliance, cleaning, new installation, unusually large load, door left open, etc.)
4. **Is there an error code, unusual smell, smoke, spark, shock, or water near electrical parts?**

### Step 3 — Apply the Safety Gate

If any red flag in Section 3 is present, **stop troubleshooting** and follow the safety response. Safety takes priority over completing the script.

### Step 4 — Follow one issue playbook

Do not run every possible test. Choose the best match to the reported symptom. Ask permission before asking the customer to touch, clean, or reposition anything.

### Step 5 — Confirm the result

Ask the customer to describe what changed. Do not mark a ticket “resolved” merely because the customer completed the steps. The appliance must return to normal operation with no safety red flags.

### Step 6 — Close or escalate

- **Resolved remotely:** Log the final check, steps that worked, and any preventive advice.
- **Not resolved:** Book an appropriate technician; include the checks already completed so they are not repeated unnecessarily.
- **Suspected major/repeat fault:** Flag for supervisor review and replacement assessment.

## 3. Safety Gate — stop-use and escalation rules

### 3.1 Immediate stop-use red flags

Tell the customer to stop using the appliance if any of the following occur:

- Smoke, flames, sparks/arcing, burning smell, melted plastic, or visible electrical damage.
- Electric shock/tingling sensation from the appliance or nearby metalwork.
- Repeated circuit-breaker trips or fuse failures.
- Water touching a plug, socket, cable, control panel, motor, or other electrical area.
- Microwave door, hinge, latch, or seal is damaged or the microwave operates abnormally with its door open.
- AC or refrigerator has strong chemical odour, suspected refrigerant leak, or abnormal icing with poor performance.
- Washing-machine leakage is substantial, near the power supply, or the machine is moving violently.
- Appliance casing is unusually hot with burning smell, visible damage, or abnormal operation.

**Customer instruction:** Do not touch wet plugs, electrical parts, or a live appliance. If safe to do so without approaching water or exposed wiring, stop the appliance and isolate power at the wall switch/breaker. If it is not safe, move away and arrange qualified assistance. In a fire or immediate danger, follow local emergency procedures.

### 3.2 Non-negotiable boundaries

Customers must never be guided to:
- Open appliance casings or remove electrical panels.
- Touch internal wiring, capacitors, PCB, compressor terminals, motors, pumps, internal valves, or microwave components.
- Charge refrigerant/gas, release refrigerant, or repair a suspected refrigerant leak.
- Use sharp objects to remove freezer or AC-coil ice.
- Insert tools, wire, high-pressure air, or chemicals into internal drains/vents.
- Lift, tilt, or move heavy appliances without trained assistance.
- Climb ladders, reach AC outdoor units, or work at a height.
- Bypass a door/lid switch, interlock, fuse, earthing, sensor, or other safety mechanism.
- Operate a microwave with a damaged door, damaged interior, persistent arcing, or without required turntable parts.

### 3.3 Priority levels

| Priority | Use when | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **P0 — Safety** | Smoke, sparks, shock, burning smell, water near electrical parts, repeated breaker trip, unsafe microwave door | Stop-use instructions; urgent qualified service/supervisor escalation. Do not continue troubleshooting. |
| **P1 — Essential appliance unavailable** | No cooling in hot conditions, refrigerator not keeping food cold, washing machine fully unusable, microwave unavailable where urgent need is reported | Same-day triage where operationally possible; set customer expectation honestly. |
| **P2 — Degraded function** | Weak cooling, slow cycle, intermittent noise, minor leak away from electrics | Safe troubleshooting, then normal service booking if unresolved. |
| **P3 — Cosmetic/minor** | Light, shelf, tray or accessory concern with no safety issue | Log and schedule based on policy/parts availability. |

Priority labels are operational guidance. Use the current RentBasket service-level policy for actual deadlines.

## 4. Universal issue-resolution and escalation rules

### 4.1 When to stop remote troubleshooting

Create a technician ticket when:
- One complete relevant playbook has been attempted and the symptom remains.
- The fix would require a tool, disassembly, an internal component check, refrigerant handling, or electrical work.
- The customer is uncomfortable, cannot safely reach the component, or instructions are unclear.
- The customer reports an error code that the model manual identifies as a service fault.
- The issue returns shortly after a remote fix or previous repair.

Do not keep asking the customer to repeat the same reset or power cycle without new evidence.

### 4.2 Replacement assessment triggers

A supervisor may assess replacement when one or more apply:
- A major component or sealed system has failed.
- The fault is safety-related or the appliance is not fit for use.
- The same fault recurs after repair or the appliance has repeated tickets in a short period.
- Parts are unavailable or repair turnaround is incompatible with the service target.
- Expected repair cost plus downtime is not economical compared with replacement.
- The unit's condition/age makes further repair low-confidence.

These triggers are **not automatic approval**. Follow RentBasket's current asset, inventory, and approval rules. Record the reason for repair versus replacement.

### 4.3 Customer-friendly close

> “We have completed the safe checks available over the phone. The issue needs a technician so we do not risk damaging the appliance or your home. I’ve recorded what we tried so the technician can continue from here.”

# PART A — AIR CONDITIONER (LLOYD / VOLTAS)

Applies to split and window ACs. Ask which type the customer has. Do not ask them to access the outdoor unit, remove electrical covers, or touch refrigerant pipes.

## AC Playbook AC-01 — AC is running but not cooling

**Typical report:** “AC chal raha hai, par cooling nahi hai.”

**Ask first**
- Is air coming out of the indoor unit?
- Is the air room-temperature, slightly cool, or completely warm?
- What mode is displayed: Cool, Fan, Dry, Auto, or another mode?
- Did cooling stop suddenly, or has it been weak for several days?
- Is there ice on the unit, water leakage, an error code, or an unusual smell?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm the remote is set to **Cool** (snowflake symbol on many models), not Fan, Auto, or a heating mode.
2. Set a reasonable test temperature below the current room temperature and choose a medium/high fan speed. Avoid repeatedly changing settings during the test.
3. Close doors and windows and ensure curtains/room conditions are not causing unusually high heat load.
4. Check that accessible air outlets are not blocked by curtains or furniture.
5. If the customer is comfortable and the manual permits it, switch the unit off, isolate power safely, clean only the accessible air filters, let them dry completely, reinstall them, then restart.
6. Allow approximately 10–15 minutes for a basic performance check; do not promise a specific cooling time for every room/model.

**Resolved when:** Cool airflow returns and the customer reports a clear improvement without any red flags.

**Escalate when:** No improvement, weak airflow after filter cleaning, ice, repeated shutdown, error code, unusual noise, or strong/chemical/burning smell.

**Technician notes:** Record indoor airflow, whether the outdoor unit appears to start (only if visible from a safe location), error code, icing, and previous gas/service history. Do not diagnose a refrigerant shortage solely from “not cooling.”

## AC Playbook AC-02 — Weak airflow

**Ask first:** Is the air volume low even at high fan speed? Is there a filter warning? Is ice visible?

**Customer steps**
1. Set fan speed to Medium/High and check that vents are not closed or obstructed.
2. If safe and allowed by the manual, clean accessible filters and dry them completely before reinstalling.
3. Check whether airflow improves after restarting the unit.

**Resolved when:** Airflow returns to normal.

**Escalate when:** Airflow remains weak, a blower/fan noise is present, the coil is frozen, or filter removal is not straightforward.

**Do not:** Ask the customer to clean coils, bend fins, spray chemicals inside the unit, or remove indoor panels.

## AC Playbook AC-03 — Water dripping from indoor unit

**Ask first:** Is the water dripping from the indoor AC, from a pipe connection, or from the wall? Is water near a socket or electrical cable? Is the AC also not cooling or icing?

**Customer steps**
1. If water is reaching electrical parts, stop immediately under the Safety Gate. Otherwise, switch the AC off to prevent further leakage and protect furniture/flooring.
2. Move belongings away and place a container/towel only if it is safe to do so.
3. Check whether the accessible filter is visibly dirty; clean it only as instructed in AC-02 and only if the unit can be safely isolated.
4. Do not detach drain pipes, open the front housing beyond routine filter access, or flush the drain.

**Resolved when:** Do not mark resolved until leakage has stopped and the unit has been checked safely. A short pause alone is not proof of repair.

**Escalate when:** Leakage is persistent, returns after restart, comes from the casing/wall, is accompanied by icing/poor cooling, or the drain/installation needs inspection.

**Technician notes:** Record leak location, quantity, whether AC was cooling, visible ice, recent installation/shift, and whether the drain outlet is visibly accessible from ground level.

## AC Playbook AC-04 — Ice or frost on the indoor unit / pipes

**Ask first:** Where is the ice visible? Is airflow weak? Is cooling poor? Is water dripping as the ice melts?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop cooling mode. If safe, switch the AC off using the normal control and allow ice to melt naturally.
2. Keep people and belongings clear of dripping water. Protect nearby electrical items.
3. Once the ice is gone, the customer may check/clean only the accessible air filter if the manual permits.
4. Do not chip, scrape, heat, or forcibly remove ice. Do not keep operating the AC to “melt” it faster.

**Escalate when:** Ice returns, cooling remains weak, water leakage persists, or ice is on inaccessible pipes/outdoor components.

**Technician notes:** Possible causes include restricted airflow, sensor/control faults or refrigerant/system issues. Diagnose on site; never assume gas charging is the fix.

## AC Playbook AC-05 — Unusual noise, rattling, grinding or vibration

**Ask first:** Indoor or outdoor source? What kind of sound? Does it happen immediately or only when the fan/compressor runs? Any burning smell?

**Customer steps**
1. If grinding, sharp metallic contact, electrical buzzing or burning smell is reported, stop use and escalate.
2. Otherwise, check from a safe position whether a curtain, loose nearby object, or furniture is touching the indoor unit.
3. Do not touch the fan, remove covers, climb to inspect an outdoor unit, or tighten mounting hardware.

**Resolved when:** A clearly external obstruction is removed and normal operation returns without noise or safety concern.

**Escalate when:** Noise persists, is new/loud, comes from a fan/compressor, or unit vibrates against its mount.

## AC Playbook AC-06 — AC switches off / keeps restarting

**Ask first:** Does the display show an error? Does the breaker trip? Does it turn off after a timer period or after running for several minutes?

**Customer steps**
1. Check remote timer, sleep mode, temperature and operating mode.
2. Check whether the displayed temperature or mode was changed accidentally.
3. If there is no safety red flag and the manual permits it, perform one normal restart after a short wait.
4. If the breaker trips, do not repeatedly reset it. Apply the Safety Gate and escalate.

**Escalate when:** Repeated shutdowns, error code, breaker trip, abnormal heat/smell, or the problem returns after one restart.

## AC Playbook AC-07 — Remote or control panel not working

**Ask first:** Is the indoor unit itself powered? Is the display lit? Does any remote button work?

**Customer steps**
1. Check the remote display and replace batteries if available and the customer is comfortable.
2. Ensure the remote is pointed at the unit and there is no obvious obstruction.
3. Confirm the unit's main power supply is on, without touching wiring or opening switchboards.
4. Try the manufacturer's documented control/lock procedure if a lock symbol is shown.

**Escalate when:** The unit has power but no control response, display shows an error, or wall controls/wiring appear faulty.

## AC Playbook AC-08 — Outdoor unit does not appear to run

**Ask first:** Is the indoor unit blowing air? Is the unit set to Cool? Has the compressor delay just started? Is there an error code?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm Cool mode and a set temperature below room temperature.
2. Wait a few minutes for any normal compressor start delay specified by the manual.
3. Observe only from a safe ground-level location whether the outdoor unit appears to run. Do not touch, open, climb to, or remove anything from the outdoor unit.

**Escalate when:** The outdoor unit remains inactive and cooling is absent, there is abnormal noise, or an error code appears.

## AC Playbook AC-09 — Bad smell from AC

**Ask first:** Musty/damp, dusty, chemical, or burning smell? Does it appear only when starting or continue throughout operation?

**Customer steps**
1. For burning, chemical, smoke, or strong unusual odour: stop the AC and apply the Safety Gate.
2. For a mild dusty/musty smell only, stop the unit and clean the accessible filter if safe and permitted; let the filter dry fully.
3. Do not spray perfume, disinfectant, or cleaner into the unit.

**Escalate when:** Smell persists, returns quickly, is chemical/burning, or is accompanied by leakage, icing, or poor cooling.

## AC Playbook AC-10 — “AC is very old” / poor performance over time

**Purpose:** This is an asset-condition complaint, not a diagnosis.

**Ask first:** What is the specific performance problem? Is there a leak, noise, poor cooling, repeated service history, or only concern about age/appearance?

**Customer steps**
1. Record the specific symptom and whether the AC has recently been serviced.
2. Run the relevant playbook (usually AC-01, AC-02, AC-05, or AC-09).
3. Do not tell the customer that age alone means the unit must be replaced.

**Escalate when:** Performance remains poor after safe checks or the service history indicates repeat faults.

**Internal action:** Have the supervisor review age/model, repair cost, repeat tickets, energy/condition concerns and replacement stock. Record repair-versus-replace rationale.

## AC Playbook AC-11 — AC works but room still feels warm

**Ask first:** Is air from the unit cool? What is the approximate room size? Are doors/windows open? Is direct sunlight or high heat load present?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm Cool mode and a reasonable setpoint.
2. Close doors/windows; reduce obvious heat entry if practical.
3. Ensure the airflow path is unobstructed and the filter is clean.
4. Allow a reasonable test period without repeatedly changing settings.

**Escalate when:** Air itself is not cool, room temperature never improves under normal conditions, or the unit repeatedly stops. Capacity/room mismatch is a technician or supervisor assessment, not something to promise remotely.

## AC Playbook AC-12 — AC has a sudden increase in water, condensation or dampness

**Ask first:** Is water draining outside normally? Is it dripping indoors? Has the weather/humidity changed? Is any wall/socket wet?

**Customer steps**
1. If indoor leakage or electrical risk is present, stop use and follow AC-03/Safety Gate.
2. Do not mistake normal condensate flowing from the drain outlet outdoors for a leak.
3. Observe the leak source from a safe position and send a photo if safe.

**Escalate when:** Water is indoors, drain flow appears abnormal, dampness is new, or the source is unclear.

# PART B — REFRIGERATOR (WHIRLPOOL / LLOYD)

Ask whether the refrigerator is **direct-cool** or **frost-free** if known. Advice can differ by design. Avoid moving, tilting, unplugging for long periods, or dismantling the unit unless the model manual or approved technician specifically directs it.

## Fridge Playbook FR-01 — Refrigerator is not cooling

**Ask first**
- Is the light/display on?
- Is the freezer also warm, or only the refrigerator compartment?
- Was the unit just installed, moved, heavily loaded, or left open?
- Any unusual clicking, burning smell, error display, or water leak?

**Customer steps**
1. If the power/display is off, check whether the plug is visibly seated and whether the wall outlet has power. Do not touch a wet or damaged plug.
2. Confirm the temperature control has not been turned to Off or an unusually warm setting.
3. Check that the door closes fully and food packages are not holding it open.
4. Ensure food is not blocking visible internal air vents. Do not remove internal panels.
5. Avoid repeated door opening and allow time for temperature recovery after a large grocery load or recent installation, following the manual.

**Resolved when:** The unit resumes cooling and maintains temperature according to its model guidance.

**Escalate when:** Both compartments remain warm, cooling is absent, there is a repeated clicking/abnormal sound, error code, or cooling does not recover after the normal stabilization period.

**Food safety:** If food may have been kept above safe temperatures for an extended period, advise the customer to follow food-safety guidance and not rely on smell alone to determine safety.

## Fridge Playbook FR-02 — Freezer cold, refrigerator compartment warm

**Ask first:** Is the freezer cold? Is the fridge area packed tightly? Is there frost/ice buildup? Is the door sealing?

**Customer steps**
1. Move food away from visible air vents inside the fridge; do not remove panels or covers.
2. Check that containers do not prevent the door from closing.
3. Check the door gasket visually for obvious food/debris and wipe it gently with a damp cloth, then dry.
4. If heavy frost is present, do not chip it away or use sharp/hot tools.

**Escalate when:** The fridge stays warm, frost returns, or an internal fan/defrost/airflow fault is suspected.

## Fridge Playbook FR-03 — Water pooling inside the refrigerator

**Ask first:** Where is the water: vegetable drawer, back wall, bottom shelf, freezer, or under the appliance? Is it a frost-free model?

**Customer steps**
1. Remove nearby food and dry the accessible water with a cloth.
2. Check for spilled liquids and containers leaking inside.
3. Ensure food is not touching/covering visible drain or air openings. Do not insert wire, toothpicks, or tools into a drain.
4. Check the door closes fully and the gasket is not obstructed.

**Escalate when:** Water returns, the drain opening is inaccessible/blocked, ice is involved, or the source is not clearly a spill.

## Fridge Playbook FR-04 — Water leaking onto the floor

**Ask first:** Is the water near the power outlet? Is it coming from inside the fridge, rear/bottom area, a water line, or an unknown location?

**Customer steps**
1. If water is near electrical connections or the plug, do not touch the wet area or plug; apply the Safety Gate.
2. If safe, protect the floor and move nearby items without moving the refrigerator.
3. Check for a spilled container or obvious door-closing issue.
4. Do not pull the refrigerator out, tilt it, detach water lines, or open the rear panel.

**Escalate when:** The leak source is not an obvious spill, any water returns, or a connection/drain needs inspection.

## Fridge Playbook FR-05 — Excessive freezer ice / frost (“freezer freeze ho raha hai”)

**Clarify first:** Does the customer mean food is freezing normally, or that a thick layer of unwanted ice/frost is building up?

**Ask:** Is the door frequently opened? Is the seal damaged? Is warm air getting in? Is it a frost-free model?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm the door closes completely and no food package obstructs it.
2. Wipe visible dirt from the door gasket using a soft damp cloth and dry it.
3. Avoid leaving the door open and avoid placing hot food inside unless the manual allows it after cooling.
4. Do not chip ice, use knives, hair dryers, heaters or boiling water, or dismantle freezer panels.
5. Do not instruct a long unplugged defrost unless the model manual and RentBasket process explicitly permit it and a safe food-storage plan is in place.

**Escalate when:** Thick frost builds again quickly, a frost-free model is icing excessively, the door seal is damaged, or cooling performance is affected.

## Fridge Playbook FR-06 — Some parts are warm / side walls feel hot

**Ask first:** Is the warmth on the exterior side/back, or are food and shelves inside warm? Is the unit cooling normally?

**Customer steps**
1. Explain that some exterior warmth can be normal heat rejection, depending on the design and operating conditions.
2. Ensure there is the model-recommended ventilation clearance and the unit is not pushed hard against an obstruction.
3. Avoid repeated door opening and confirm that the temperature control is appropriate.

**Escalate when:** Interior food is warm, cooling is poor, the cabinet becomes unusually hot with abnormal smell/noise, or there are signs of electrical danger.

## Fridge Playbook FR-07 — Bad smell inside the refrigerator

**Ask first:** Is the smell from food/spill, drain area, plastic/chemical source, or burning/electrical source?

**Customer steps**
1. If burning, chemical, smoke or electrical smell: stop use and apply the Safety Gate.
2. Otherwise, remove spoiled food and clean removable shelves/drawers with mild, appliance-safe cleaning methods.
3. Wipe accessible spills and dry all cleaned parts before returning them.
4. Store strong-smelling food in sealed containers and inspect for a leaking container.

**Escalate when:** Odour persists after cleaning, seems to come from the rear/motor area, or is accompanied by heat, clicking, or poor cooling.

## Fridge Playbook FR-08 — Loud noise, rattling, buzzing or clicking

**Ask first:** Is it a light hum, vibration/rattle, repeated clicking, grinding, or a sudden new sound?

**Customer steps**
1. If burning smell, smoke, shock or repeated breaker trip: stop use and apply Safety Gate.
2. Check from outside that the refrigerator is not touching the wall/cabinet and that accessible loose items on top are not rattling.
3. Do not pull the refrigerator out or access the rear compressor/fan area.

**Escalate when:** Noise is loud/new/persistent, grinding is heard, clicks repeat with poor cooling, or fan/compressor service may be needed.

## Fridge Playbook FR-09 — Food freezing in the refrigerator compartment

**Ask first:** Are items touching the rear wall or air outlet? Is the setting unusually cold? Does it affect the whole compartment or one small area?

**Customer steps**
1. Check the temperature setting and adjust only within the model's recommended range.
2. Move food away from the rear wall and air vents.
3. Avoid placing temperature-sensitive items directly against cooling outlets.
4. Allow the temperature to stabilize before judging the result.

**Escalate when:** Food continues freezing despite correct settings and placement, or there is a sensor/control concern.

## Fridge Playbook FR-10 — Door not closing / gasket not sealing

**Ask first:** Is food, a shelf, or a drawer preventing closure? Is the gasket visibly dirty, loose or torn?

**Customer steps**
1. Reposition food packages and drawers so they do not protrude.
2. Wipe accessible gasket surfaces with a soft damp cloth and dry them.
3. Close the door gently and confirm it sits flush.
4. Do not use heat, glue, tools or improvised repairs on the gasket/hinge.

**Escalate when:** The gasket is torn/loose, the door sags, hinge is damaged, or the seal still fails.

## Fridge Playbook FR-11 — Fridge seems to run continuously

**Ask first:** Is it cooling? Was it recently installed, moved, heavily loaded, or left open? Is the kitchen unusually warm or ventilation restricted?

**Customer steps**
1. Check the temperature setting, door seal and whether food blocks closure.
2. Ensure the unit has the ventilation clearance specified by its manual.
3. Reduce prolonged door opening and allow recovery after large loads.

**Escalate when:** Continuous running persists with poor cooling, abnormal noise/heat, or a suspected compressor/control fault.

## Fridge Playbook FR-12 — Interior light/display not working

**Ask first:** Is the refrigerator still cooling? Is the whole appliance off, or only the light/display?

**Customer steps**
1. Check visible power connection only if dry and safe.
2. Close and reopen the door once to see whether the light responds.
3. Do not remove light covers or attempt wiring/bulb replacement unless the exact model manual explicitly identifies a user-replaceable part and RentBasket approves the task.

**Escalate when:** The appliance has no power, only the light/display fails, or internal access is required.

## Fridge Playbook FR-13 — Refrigerator appears not to start / display off

**Ask first:** Is the outlet working? Is there a recent power cut? Any burning smell, damaged cable, or wet floor?

**Customer steps**
1. Run the Safety Gate first.
2. If dry and undamaged, confirm the plug is seated and check whether household power is available using a safe method (for example, another known-working small appliance in the same outlet only if appropriate and safe).
3. Do not use a damaged extension cord or open the appliance.

**Escalate when:** Power is available but the refrigerator remains off, breaker trips, or the cord/outlet is damaged.

# PART C — WASHING MACHINE (WHIRLPOOL)

First identify **top-load, front-load, or semi-automatic**. Do not assume the same buttons, lid/door lock behaviour, filter access, or cycle duration across models. Never instruct customers to open a service filter or drain filter unless the exact model manual explains how and the area can be handled safely; water may spill.

## WM Playbook WM-01 — Drum does not rotate / machine does not spin

**Ask first:** Does the machine power on? Does water enter? Is there an error code? Does the drum move during wash but not spin?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the cycle and check whether the load is badly unbalanced or too large.
2. Redistribute the clothing evenly and remove a portion if overloaded.
3. Confirm the lid/door is fully closed and no clothing is trapped at the seal.
4. Select the correct wash/spin program according to the model's manual and retry once.

**Escalate when:** The drum remains stationary, a motor/burning smell is present, there is a repeated error code, or a loud mechanical noise occurs.

**Never instruct:** Customers must not try to rotate the drum by force, bypass the lid/door lock, or open the casing.

## WM Playbook WM-02 — Water not entering / inlet valve complaint

**Ask first:** Is household water available? Is the tap feeding the machine open? Does the machine show an inlet/error code? Is the visible hose bent or leaking?

**Customer steps**
1. Pause/turn off the machine. If water is leaking, close the water tap first if safe.
2. Confirm the household water supply is on and the machine's accessible supply tap is open.
3. Visually check the inlet hose for sharp bends, kinks or visible damage. Do not pull the machine out to reach it.
4. If the hose is visibly kinked and readily accessible, straighten it gently without disconnecting it.
5. Restart once according to the manual.

**Escalate when:** Water supply is normal but water does not enter, the hose/connector is damaged, there is a leak at the connection, or internal inlet-valve/filter work is needed.

**Key rule:** Customer may check the external tap and visible hose only. Do not direct them to dismantle or clean the internal inlet valve or inlet filter.

## WM Playbook WM-03 — Slow cycle / cycle taking too long

**Ask first:** Which program is selected? Is it a quick wash or a long/heavy cycle? Is water entering/draining normally? Is there an error code?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm the selected program and the approximate duration in the model's manual/display.
2. Check for an overloaded or unbalanced drum; redistribute clothes if necessary.
3. Observe whether the machine is paused waiting for water, draining, or balancing the load.
4. Do not repeatedly cancel long cycles without understanding the program.

**Escalate when:** The timer is frozen, the cycle repeatedly stalls, water will not enter/drain, or an error code occurs.

## WM Playbook WM-04 — Water leaking from inlet hose or connection

**Ask first:** Is water leaking from the tap connection, hose body, machine inlet, or underneath the machine? Is water near an electrical outlet?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the cycle. If safe, close the water supply tap.
2. If water is near the plug/socket, do not touch wet electrical areas; apply Safety Gate.
3. Contain water with towels only if safe and keep people away from the wet area.
4. Take a photo from a safe position; do not tighten fittings with tools or disconnect hoses.

**Escalate when:** Leakage persists, the hose is cracked/bulging, the connection is damaged, or the source is underneath/inaccessible.

## WM Playbook WM-05 — Machine not draining / water left in drum

**Ask first:** Is the drain hose visibly kinked? Is the machine showing a drain error? Is water leaking onto the floor?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the machine if water is leaking.
2. From an accessible position, inspect the visible drain hose for kinks or crushing.
3. Ensure the hose outlet has not visibly slipped out of its intended drain point, without handling contaminated water or moving the machine.
4. Do not ask the customer to open a pump filter, remove panels, or manually drain heavy water unless the exact manual and approved safety procedure support it.

**Escalate when:** Hose path looks normal but water remains, an internal filter/pump blockage is suspected, or the problem repeats.

## WM Playbook WM-06 — Clothes remain very wet after a cycle

**Ask first:** Did the cycle include a spin? Was a single heavy item or a very small/large load used? Did the machine show an imbalance or drain error?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm the program includes spin and that spin speed is not set to No Spin/low spin.
2. Redistribute the load evenly and remove excess clothing if overloaded.
3. Check the visible drain hose for an obvious kink.
4. Run one appropriate spin/drain program as instructed by the manual.

**Escalate when:** Machine cannot spin, water remains, error code persists, or clothes remain wet after a correctly selected spin cycle.

## WM Playbook WM-07 — Excessive shaking, walking or banging

**Ask first:** Is the machine newly installed or moved? Does the sound occur only during spin? Is the load one heavy item or badly unbalanced?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the cycle if the machine is moving violently or could damage surroundings.
2. Redistribute the load and reduce overloading.
3. Check that accessible feet/floor contact appear stable; do not tilt or lift the machine to adjust it.
4. Do not remove shipping bolts or adjust levelling feet unless the model manual explicitly describes the customer task and RentBasket approves it.

**Escalate when:** Vibration persists with a balanced load, the appliance shifts, or there is banging/grinding suggesting internal or installation issues.

## WM Playbook WM-08 — Machine does not start

**Ask first:** Is the display lit? Is there a lock icon? Is the door/lid completely closed? Has the breaker tripped?

**Customer steps**
1. If smoke, burning smell, shock, or repeated breaker trip is present, apply Safety Gate.
2. Check the power connection only if dry and undamaged.
3. Ensure the door/lid is fully closed and no clothing is trapped.
4. Check child/control lock using the exact model's manual.
5. Cancel a previously selected program and select a new one only as the manual instructs.

**Escalate when:** Power is available but there is no response, the lock does not engage, an error code persists, or the breaker trips.

## WM Playbook WM-09 — Detergent residue / too much foam

**Ask first:** Was the recommended detergent type and amount used? Is the detergent designed for this machine type? Is the load oversized?

**Customer steps**
1. Check the manual for the recommended detergent type and dosage.
2. Avoid adding extra detergent to compensate for poor cleaning.
3. Clean only the removable detergent drawer if the manual identifies it as user-removable.
4. Run an appropriate rinse cycle if the manual permits and no water leakage/safety issue exists.

**Escalate when:** Water does not drain, residue/foam repeatedly remains despite correct use, or the drawer/dispensing system is damaged.

## WM Playbook WM-10 — Bad smell from drum or seal

**Ask first:** Is the smell musty, sewage-like, chemical, or burning? Is water left inside after a cycle?

**Customer steps**
1. For burning/electrical or chemical smell, stop use and apply Safety Gate.
2. For a mild musty smell, leave the door/lid open for ventilation only if safe and permitted by the household setup.
3. Wipe accessible seals/surfaces and clean the removable drawer according to the manual.
4. Use the model's documented drum-clean/tub-clean program if present; follow the manual on whether detergent is needed.

**Escalate when:** Smell persists, water stays in the drum, the drain appears blocked, or an internal cleaning/service task is required.

## WM Playbook WM-11 — Error code or machine stops mid-cycle

**Ask first:** What exact code or indicator is shown? At what stage does it stop? Is there water in the drum, a leak, or an unusual sound?

**Customer steps**
1. Record the code exactly, including letters, numbers and flashing pattern; ask for a photo if possible.
2. Consult the exact model manual or approved support reference for the code.
3. Only perform a reset if the model manual recommends it and no safety red flag exists.
4. Do not guess the meaning of a code based on another Whirlpool model.

**Escalate when:** The code identifies a service fault, persists after the documented basic check, or recurs after reset.

## WM Playbook WM-12 — Door/lid will not unlock or close

**Ask first:** Is the cycle running or is water still in the drum? Is a lock symbol displayed? Is clothing trapped?

**Customer steps**
1. Follow the normal Pause/Cancel procedure in the exact model manual and wait for any documented safety delay.
2. If water remains, do not force the door/lid open.
3. Check for visible clothing obstruction only; do not insert tools into the lock.

**Escalate when:** The door/lid remains locked, hinge/latch is damaged, or access would require an emergency release or panel removal.

# PART D — MICROWAVE (HAIER / PANASONIC / MIDEA)

Microwaves contain high-voltage components that can remain hazardous even when unplugged. Customer troubleshooting is limited to external power checks, controls, safe cookware, door closure, and user-accessible cleaning. **Never instruct a customer to open the microwave casing.**

## MW Playbook MW-01 — Microwave has no power / display is blank

**Ask first:** Is the socket working? Is the plug/cable damaged? Did the breaker trip? Any burning smell or spark?

**Customer steps**
1. Apply Safety Gate for damaged cable/plug, burning smell, smoke, shock, or repeated breaker trips.
2. If dry and undamaged, confirm the plug is fully seated in a suitable outlet recommended by the manual.
3. Check whether household power is available; do not use a damaged extension cord or overload a shared outlet.
4. Check for a display/standby or child-lock feature described by the exact manual.
5. Perform a simple power reset only if the manual recommends it and there are no safety red flags.

**Escalate when:** The display remains blank despite safe external checks, breaker trips, or any damage is seen.

## MW Playbook MW-02 — Microwave runs but food does not heat

**Ask first:** Is the timer counting down? Is the cavity light/fan/turntable active? Which mode and power level are selected? Is Demo mode shown, if the model has it?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm the door closes securely and the chosen program is a microwave cooking/heating mode, not timer, grill-only, convection-only, or another unsuitable mode.
2. Confirm power level and cooking time as appropriate for the food and manual.
3. If there is no smoke, spark, unusual noise or damage, test with a microwave-safe cup/bowl of water for about one minute on a suitable high-power setting. Never run the microwave empty.
4. Stop if the water does not warm, if an error code appears, or if any unusual smell/noise occurs.

**Escalate when:** It still does not heat, an error code appears, or the appliance behaves abnormally. A failed heating test is a service case, not a customer repair task.

## MW Playbook MW-03 — Sparks/arcing inside cavity

**Ask first:** Did the customer use metal/foil, metallic trim, a twist tie, or a damaged container? Is there visible cavity coating damage or a burn mark?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop cooking immediately. Do not continue the test.
2. Once safe, switch off/unplug only if it is safe to reach the plug without approaching sparks, smoke, or a damaged socket.
3. After the unit is cool and isolated, remove inappropriate cookware/metal only if safe and inspect visually for obvious food residue or damage; do not scrape cavity surfaces.
4. Do not reuse the microwave if arcing recurs or if the cavity coating/door is damaged.

**Escalate when:** Sparks recur, the cavity is damaged, or the source is not clearly an inappropriate item. Treat smoke, burning, or damaged door/interlock as P0.

## MW Playbook MW-04 — Turntable not rotating / tray wobbling

**Ask first:** Is the unit otherwise heating? Is the tray overloaded or is a container touching the cavity wall?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the microwave before handling the tray.
2. When cool and safe, remove the glass tray and roller ring if they are user-removable, clean food debris from the accessible floor, and reposition them correctly according to the manual.
3. Reduce the load and centre the food/container; ensure it does not touch the cavity walls.
4. Do not operate without the required tray/roller parts or if the tray is cracked.

**Escalate when:** Turntable still fails to rotate, the roller ring/drive part is broken, or abnormal grinding/noise continues.

## MW Playbook MW-05 — Buttons or touch panel not responding

**Ask first:** Is a lock symbol displayed? Does the display work? Did liquid spill onto the panel?

**Customer steps**
1. Check the child/control lock procedure in the exact manual.
2. If the panel is wet, stop use, isolate power only if safe, and allow safe external drying; do not spray liquid into panel edges.
3. If the manual permits, perform one normal reset.

**Escalate when:** Controls remain unresponsive, display flickers abnormally, or the panel was exposed to liquid internally.

## MW Playbook MW-06 — Door will not close, latch, or appears damaged

**Ask first:** Is food/debris obstructing closure? Is the hinge, latch, seal or glass visibly damaged?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop use if closure is not secure or any door part appears damaged.
2. Check for a harmless external obstruction around the door edge; do not force the door.
3. Wipe visible food debris from accessible surfaces with a soft cloth once the unit is isolated and cool.

**Escalate when:** Door, hinge, latch, seal or glass is damaged, or the microwave does not reliably recognize the closed door. Never bypass an interlock.

## MW Playbook MW-07 — Unusual noise / grinding / vibration

**Ask first:** Does the sound come from the turntable, fan, or seem electrical? Did the noise start suddenly?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop use for grinding, electrical buzzing, burning smell, or a sudden abnormal sound.
2. If safe and the unit is cool, check that the tray and roller ring are correctly seated and that cookware is not touching the cavity walls.
3. Do not open the casing or attempt to lubricate internal parts.

**Escalate when:** Noise persists after tray repositioning or appears to come from internal fan/motor/electrical components.

## MW Playbook MW-08 — Burning smell, smoke or microwave will not stop

**Ask first:** Is there smoke/flame? Is the unit still running? Is the door closed? Is the smell electrical or from food?

**Customer steps**
1. Stop the appliance immediately using the Stop/Cancel control if safe. If smoke/fire/electrical danger is present, do not approach the unit; follow local emergency procedures.
2. Isolate power only if safe. Do not touch a hot or sparking plug/socket.
3. Do not use the microwave again until inspected after any electrical smell, smoke, or uncontrolled operation.
4. Never instruct the customer to open the door if that would expose them to flame/smoke; follow emergency guidance.

**Escalate:** P0 urgent safety escalation. Do not continue normal troubleshooting.

## MW Playbook MW-09 — Uneven or slow heating

**Ask first:** Is the correct heating mode and power level selected? Is the food/container arranged correctly? Is the dish microwave-safe?

**Customer steps**
1. Confirm microwave mode, power level and time from the manual/recipe.
2. Use microwave-safe cookware and arrange food evenly; stir/rotate food manually if the instructions for the food call for it.
3. Avoid metal, foil, and containers with metallic trim unless the exact manual explicitly permits a specific accessory.
4. If heating is unusually weak across different food items, use MW-02 once; do not keep testing if abnormal behaviour is observed.

**Escalate when:** Heating stays weak after correct settings, or abnormal noise/error/odour accompanies it.

## MW Playbook MW-10 — Error code or display message

**Ask first:** What exact code appears? When does it appear? Does the unit continue running?

**Customer steps**
1. Photograph the code and identify the exact model.
2. Check the model-specific manual/support page for the meaning.
3. Try only the documented basic recovery step if the code does not indicate a safety/service fault and no red flag exists.

**Escalate when:** Code persists, is identified as a service fault, or the model-specific instructions recommend service. Never infer a fault code from another model or brand.

# PART E — TECHNICIAN AND REPLACEMENT PLAYBOOKS

## TECH-01 — When booking a technician

Before assigning, capture:
- Appliance/category/brand/model and rental asset ID.
- Exact symptom and when it occurs.
- Red flags asked and customer answers.
- Playbook ID and all steps attempted, including results.
- Photos/video and error code, if safe.
- Whether the customer has stopped using the appliance.
- Access details and availability window.
- Suspected skill required (AC/refrigeration sealed system, appliance electrical/mechanical, microwave service, installation/drainage).

Assign based on demonstrated capability, not just general technician availability. Confirm technician can safely perform the expected work. As noted by operations, **Digamber cannot perform AC or refrigerator gas charging**; route refrigerant diagnosis/repair to an appropriately qualified and equipped service partner, such as Pankaj, Manoj, or another approved technician as appropriate. Do not assume any named technician is qualified for every task without confirmation.

## TECH-02 — Technician visit checklist

Technician records:
1. Arrival time and customer availability.
2. Reproduced symptom: yes/no.
3. Confirmed diagnosis and evidence.
4. Safety issues/condition on arrival.
5. Repair attempted, parts used, and old parts disposition where applicable.
6. Functional test completed and result.
7. Advice given to customer.
8. Whether the ticket is repaired, awaiting parts, unresolved, or recommended for replacement.
9. Whether the same complaint has returned from a previous visit.
10. Estimated return-to-service time if not fixed.

For AC/refrigerator refrigerant system work, the technician should diagnose the cause and address any leak before charging; charging alone is not a durable repair when a leak remains.

## TECH-03 — Replacement assessment

Replacement reviewer checks:
- Safety or major-component concern.
- Asset age/condition and service history.
- Same issue recurring after repair.
- Repair and parts cost, technician availability, logistics, and customer downtime.
- Replacement inventory and delivery timeline.
- Whether the issue is customer-usage/installation/external-supply related versus appliance failure, without making unsupported assumptions.
- Approval under RentBasket's current policy.

Record one reason code:
- `REPLACE_SAFETY`
- `REPLACE_MAJOR_FAILURE`
- `REPLACE_REPEAT_FAILURE`
- `REPLACE_UNECONOMICAL_REPAIR`
- `REPLACE_PARTS_UNAVAILABLE`
- `REPAIR_APPROVED`
- `NO_FAULT_FOUND`
- `CUSTOMER_USAGE_GUIDANCE`

These code names are suggested for a future ticketing system; align them with existing software if codes already exist.

## 7. Quality rules for call-centre agents

- Read the safety questions before troubleshooting any symptom.
- Do not over-diagnose from a customer's description. “No cooling” is a symptom, not proof of a gas issue or compressor failure.
- Ask one question at a time; do not overwhelm the customer with a long checklist.
- Explain why a step is being requested and ask for confirmation before continuing.
- Use the model manual when a procedure may differ across variants.
- Do not mark resolved until the customer reports normal operation.
- Capture negative results too. “Filter cleaned; no improvement” helps the technician avoid repeating work.
- If the customer is elderly, disabled, alone, uncomfortable, or unable to reach the component safely, skip the self-help step and escalate.
- Never suggest that customer self-service is a condition for receiving help from RentBasket.
- Never promise an exact visit or replacement time until dispatch/inventory confirms it.
- Do not say “the gas is low,” “the compressor is gone,” “the inlet valve is faulty,” or similar without an appropriate diagnosis.

# PART G — IMPLEMENTATION NOTES FOR CURSOR / PRODUCT TEAM

If this SOP is being converted into a support tool or WhatsApp bot:

1. Store playbooks as structured content with `appliance_type`, `brand_scope`, `issue_code`, `safety_gate`, `questions`, `steps`, `resolved_criteria`, `escalation_criteria`, and `technician_notes`.
2. Put the Safety Gate before any appliance-specific instructions; if a P0 flag is true, suppress all regular troubleshooting steps.
3. Show one step at a time and capture the customer's response (`yes`, `no`, `not_sure`, free-text, or photo where supported).
4. Provide “I can't do this safely” and “Skip to technician” options at every customer step.
5. Stop the playbook immediately when the success criterion is met or an escalation rule is triggered.
6. Never let generative AI invent model-specific procedures, error-code meanings, service promises, charges, or replacement approvals. Use the exact manual/reference or hand off to a human.
7. Capture language preference; offer plain English or Hinglish scripts. Keep the meaning and safety constraints unchanged when translating.
8. Add configuration for service hours, technician roster, capability matrix, visit SLAs, replacement approvals, spare-part availability, and customer notifications rather than hardcoding them in the playbook content.
9. Keep a human override and audit log for safety escalation, technician assignment, and replacement decisions.
10. Review playbooks quarterly and whenever manufacturer guidance, appliance models, or RentBasket policy changes.
